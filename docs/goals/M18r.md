Status: in progress
Milestone: **Bots on the simulation** (`games/shooters_edge/bots`, on `engine/ai`, after M18o and M18f): a bot is a client of the server like any player, its only output a usercmd a frame through the same move and the same game program, so it can do nothing a player cannot and every improvement to the simulation is one to the bots; navigation generated from the map on `ai/path` (where a player can walk, jump, fall, swim and ride), sight and hearing on `ai/sight` over the hull trace, aim and movement on `ai/hands` with skill profiles, item timing and fights chosen on `ai/choose`, the strafe-jump and the rocket jump as moves the bot plans; every decision recorded for `ge why`, humanness measured against people's input logs with `ai/human`; `ge bot` to run, compare and sweep them
Exit: Eight bots play a match on a free map for ten minutes with a golden digest; their moves and aim pass the humanness bands; a change to a bot shows its before and after in frags, deaths, items and humanness
Labs: `se-nav`, `se-pilot`, `se-senses`, `se-bots`, `se-team`, `se-server`, `se-moves`, `se-hop`, `se-kills`, `humanness`

After M18o (the loop) and M18f (the server's client slots): bots that play through the
simulation exactly as a player does, a usercmd a frame and nothing else, on `engine/ai`'s paths,
sight, hands and choosing, with every decision recorded for `ge why` and humanness measured
against people's input logs. The design is in `docs/shooters-edge.md` § Playing together.

Built in six steps, each its own pull request:

- **M18r1, navigation proven by the move** (`plugins/shooter/nav.hpp`): **done.** The floor
  sampled on a 32-unit grid off the map's own hulls, every link from a node proven by running
  `player_move` from it (walk, drop, or a jump on the move at full, half or a quarter speed, one
  flight with room to run up), movers left out and tagged on the nodes and links they close, the
  quickest route on `ai/path`'s A* over the proofs' times. Held by `tests/shooter_nav_test.cpp`
  on the nav course (`games/shooters_edge/sim/nav_course.hpp`: stairs, a crate, a pit, a ramp and
  a ramp too steep to stand on) and the test arena's doorway, its bits by
  `tests/golden/shooter_nav.txt`; pictured by the `se-nav` lab. A standing jump is not a kind:
  its 30 u/s of air control carries a player less than the 16 units its box needs to get over an
  edge, so it reaches nothing a moving jump does not. Riding a mover (a plat) waits for M18r2,
  which reads the mover's place at run time.
- **M18r2, the pilot** (`plugins/shooter/pilot.hpp`): **done.** A route played one usercmd at a
  time through `ai/hands`, the view's yaw and the movement axes on the protocol's grid, the jump a
  button: pure pursuit along the level walks, each jump link run at from a run-up point behind its
  start and pressed on its line at its proven speed, the strafe jump on long straight stretches
  (the view along the velocity, the side axis full), waiting stepped clear of a door that closes a
  link (and barring it after `wait_s`), riding the arena's plat (the graph now places a ridden
  mover at its travel's end and proves the ways off its top there), the stuck and lost checks
  planning again. Every decision on `ai::Why`. On the server it fills a bot slot through the
  command-source seam (`pilot_source`). Held by `tests/shooter_pilot_test.cpp` on the nav course
  (every route in about its proven time, human hands arrive, the strafe faster than a walk) and the
  arena (through the door, up the plat, the door barred, with both hands), its bits by
  `tests/golden/shooter_pilot.txt`; pictured by `se-pilot`. Open: human hands loop wide at
  corners (the pilot does not yet slow for one) and their forward axis reverses about 135 times a
  minute against a person's 120, both for a later pass with M18r5's `ge bot compare`; the
  pitch waits for aim (M18r3).
- **M18r3, senses and aim** (`plugins/shooter/senses.hpp`, `aim.hpp`, `bot.hpp`): **done.**
  A bot knows only what its client is sent (`client_view`: every entity with a model, every other
  player, the frame's sounds within `kHearAt / attenuation`; the box-brush maps have no PVS, so
  nothing is cut yet), sees a player inside its view's cone with a clear `trace_move` line to the
  head or the middle (a shut door hides), hears the sounds it is sent, and remembers both on
  `ai/memory`, forgetting after six seconds. It aims on two `ai/hands` axes (yaw and pitch) under
  four skill profiles (perfect, expert, average, novice: delay, wander, settle and slew), leading
  the target by its seen velocity, and keeps running its pilot's route while it looks (the movement
  axes turned under the aim's view, `turn_axes`). Every sense change and the aim's wanted angles on
  `ai::Why`. Held by `tests/shooter_senses_test.cpp` (what is sent, the cone, the door and the
  wall, hearing's reach, the median error ordered by skill on two seeds, a human reaction, the
  route kept), its bits by `tests/golden/shooter_senses.txt`; pictured by `se-senses`. Open: the
  native rules make sounds only on fire, so hearing is unit-tested and waits for M18r4's fights on
  the baseline mod to be heard in play; the pilot's strafe is dropped while aiming.
- **M18r4, fights and items** on the game's scripted baseline mod (M18d)
  (`games/shooters_edge/bots/fighter.hpp`, `fight_run.hpp`): **done, but for the rocket jump.**
  A fighter is the M18r3 bot choosing, on `ai/choose`, from what a client has: its own stats as
  the server sends them, the map's items off the map's entity text (a client loads the map), each
  one timed from the moment it stops being sent (20 s, a weapon 30 s), and the others through its
  senses. Every tenth of a second it picks its mode (`ai::enter`: roam, fight, chase, retreat), its
  goal (`ai::pick_best`: each item's worth to it now over the time to have it, the goal held
  against a near tie) and its weapon (`ai::pick_best` by range, sent as the impulse); every command
  whether to fire (the view within the target's width at its range of the aim, in range; rockets
  and grenades at the feet, a projectile led by its flight). The senses now drop a dead player (the
  protocol's dead flag). `run_fight` plays four fighters on the baseline mod compiled from its
  sources over the arena; held by `tests/shooters_edge_fight_test.cpp` (every fighter scores, dies,
  picks up, switches and holds a better weapon, all four modes, no fault, the expert pair outscoring
  the novice pair on two seeds), its bits by `tests/golden/shooters_edge_fight.txt`; pictured by
  `se-bots`. Measured: about 27 frags a minute among four; over seeds 1 to 3 the experts took 99
  frags to the novices' 67. Open: the rocket jump (**M18r4b**, a move the pilot plans through the
  nav's proofs with the blast's push); items are taken about 30 times in two minutes, fewer than
  players manage, since most are worth nothing to a full-health player and the fights keep
  breaking the route.
- **M18r7, the team game** (`games/shooters_edge/bots/team.hpp`, `net/classic_team.hpp`):
  **first pass done.** Two teams of four fighters, each at the controls of a classic client of
  our server over the simulated network, play the baseline mod with `teamplay 1` (no damage to a
  teammate, a backpack with the victim's ammunition and weapon on every death) while the server
  records the match as a multiview demo. A team plans every half second, the way a team plays:
  its strongest player holds the map's best armour and the area round it (a leash, not a post),
  the two nearest the quad rush it from twelve seconds before it is due, whoever has the quad
  carries it to the enemy last called out, a weak player falls back to the health near the held
  area, a teammate's claim leaves an item to him, a backpack in view is taken when ammunition is
  short, and no one fires with a teammate within 40 units of the line. The fighters now read the
  map's buttons (a door's button touched or shot first, the button pressed from the side the line
  meets it), reach every item only where a route leads from where they stand, and walk the last
  step onto an item; the nav graph gains the map's teleporters (`nav_add_links`), its plats at
  their rest, its pools closed to entry (`nav_close_entries`), and a way out of the water
  (`plugins/shooter/swim.hpp`: a breadth-first field over the wet cells to the dry nodes, swum
  with the view pitched and jump held), and a dive into a pocket only the water reaches (the
  same field built from the pocket's side: a fighter wanting an item there walks to the pool's
  edge and swims in). Held by `tests/shooters_edge_team_test.cpp` (a minute on the fixture halls:
  both teams score, no team kill, every role, the recording opens with all eight players, a match
  replays to the bit; `tests/golden/shooters_edge_team.txt`) and `tests/shooter_swim_test.cpp`;
  pictured by `se-team`, which plays the shareware pack's first episode's second map with
  `--basedir`. Measured on that map, ten minutes: 79 frags (39 to 40), ten quads taken by both
  teams, the rocket launcher dived for and held by four players, a 22 MB recording. The fight
  test now sums the experts' frags over four seeds (one seed is a coin toss): 140 to the novices'
  99. Open: the frag rate is about half of people's (the aim wastes shots at range with the
  scattergun), the armour behind the shot button is not yet taken on that map, and deaths exceed
  frags (falls and drowning count against the dead).
- **M18r8, team talk and map knowledge** (`games/shooters_edge/bots/knowledge.hpp`, `team.hpp`):
  **done.** A fighter knows the map's items by its skill: an expert knows every item and times
  the key ones (the best armours, the mega health, the rocket launcher, the quad, the shield) once
  he has seen one taken; an average player knows every item but times only the small ones, so a
  key item he saw go is guessed back late; a novice knows none at the start, goes for what he
  sees, and recalls an item only after three separate sightings (24 of 57 by the end of ten
  minutes on e1m2). Belief comes from sight alone. A team talks over `say_team` (the server now
  sends a team's words to its own, and the recording keeps them): "quad in 10, get quad!" from
  whoever timed it, "going quad", "have quad!", "lost quad", "enemy at RA" (a sighting is the
  team's only once called out), "need help" (a strong free member answers), "took RA", "RA in
  20". A walk along a pool's floor turns up and out after six seconds under, and a dive once in
  the water is swum to its end. Measured on e1m2, ten minutes: 79 frags (43 to 36), no drowning
  (13 before), about 18 team messages a player. Open: the rocket launcher's pocket is reached
  only by a dive whose last metre (out of the water onto its ledge) fails, and the armour behind
  the shot button sits in an alcove no route proves a way into.
- **M18r9, minds of their own, team message keys and place names** (`bots/mind.hpp`,
  `bots/talk.hpp`, `plugins/shooter/locs.hpp`): **done.** No shared planner: each bot reads the
  team lines its own client is sent (a person's too) and plans its role alone. The messages are a
  team game's message keys (report, took, safe, lost, help, coming, point, enemy, have, get,
  over, timers), read back in the spellings people type. Reading by skill: an expert reads every
  line, an average player about half of the lines said once and nearly every repeat, a novice one
  in six and nothing in a fight; timers are exact for an expert and a few seconds out for an
  average player. The opening takes on the nearest of the launcher, the best armour (the mega
  health on a map whose armour no way reaches) and the quad, called `coming X`, then `safe [X]`;
  the quad is called by whoever timed it and repeated while no mate is near it; an item of no use
  to its finder, or the carrier's due, is pointed at for a teammate. Place names come from the
  game folder's `locs/<map>.loc`, or off the map's items. Measured on e1m2 with its community
  place names, ten minutes: 103 frags (51 to 52), one drowning, about 39 team messages a player,
  980 lines read and 892 missed across the eight. A dive that cannot finish is given up: in the
  water 3 s longer than its planned swim, or short of air with the way back shorter than the way
  on, it turns back and that dive is not tried for 30 s. Over ten seeds on e1m2 the bots had
  spent 2990 s in the water on dives (bobbing under the launcher's ledge, up to 821 s a match);
  with the give-up 96 s, and 910 frags against 789. Open: two matches in ten jam players inside
  each other at the teleporter's exit by the launcher (800 368 312), with or without the give-up:
  the mod's teleporter does not kill whoever stands on the exit.
- **M18r10, ears, a map of the team, area keeping and buttons** (`plugins/shooter/hearing.hpp`,
  `bots/ears.hpp`, `bots/spam.hpp`, `bots/buttons.hpp`): **done.** Sounds are heard by skill (an
  expert everything and every sample's meaning, the distance to a tenth; an average player close
  and unique sounds; a novice close ones only, no meanings); a pickup heard starts its item's timer
  and places its taker, a spawn places a fresh enemy. The mega health is timed only by an expert
  who took it, from his own health back at 100. An expert keeps teammates' places for ten seconds
  and reads a key area none of them is in as lost; an average player goes by `lost` lines. An
  expert holder with 20+ rockets fires at teleporter exits and power spawns while his item is
  away. Experts rate key items by scarcity on the map. Help only over a key area being lost;
  novices say only their status. Buttons come off the map's entities (shot when health is over 0):
  dm2's 200 armour, e1m2's 150, cmt4's pentagram door and cmt3's bridge are found; novices poke at
  any button. The baseline mod plays the spawn's fog sound and telefrags on a taken spawn point.
  Measured on e1m2 (ten minutes, its community place names): 11.5 frags a minute, 1048 sounds
  taken in, 65 items heard taken, 27 spawns heard. Open: dm4's bots burn in its lava, the 4v4's
  rocket spam and retakes are rare where launchers are scarce, the dm2 graph leaves most items
  unrouted. Next: M18r11, prediction and hit rates (shots at heard enemies, tracers corrected by
  the miss, grenade bounces, the hit-rate bands per weapon and skill).
- **M18r11a, temperaments** (`plugins/shooter/temper.hpp`, `bots/temper.hpp`): **done.**
  Aggressive, balanced and coward, each taking a fight by might against might (body times arms) and
  the skill's edge; the enemy's might read off what is heard of him (weapons fired, armour, mega
  and quad taken, pain), fading in 30 s. Outmatched bots turn for items away from the enemy or
  their mates; aggressive bots never hold or give items up. Measured on the arena: aggressive
  novices die about 25% more than cowardly ones and fight over four times their share; experts
  aggressive, balanced and coward made 49, 41 and 33 frags. Next: the personality traits (each
  1 to 10, novice to expert as presets over them), then M18r11's prediction and hit rates.
- **M18r11b, personalities** (`plugins/shooter/traits.hpp`): **done.** Fifteen traits from 1 to
  10 (aim per weapon, reaction, timing, knowledge, camping, discipline, moves, hearing,
  communication, teamplay, aggression), the skills and temperaments presets over them that play
  bit for bit as before (every golden unchanged); the aim's hands taken up anew per weapon. Labs
  take `--traits`. Open: camping only drives the expert holder's rockets at exits (the camp spots
  themselves wait for the kill map's gold spots); discipline only the rash chase.
- **M18r11c, reactions per sighting** (`plugins/shooter/aim.hpp` `draw_reaction`, `bot.hpp`):
  **done.** A reaction drawn per sighting, right-skewed (trait 8 round 160 ms, 5 round 230, 2
  round 300), the view held and no shot until it is over; the hands' lag cut to the tracking lag
  inside it; half the draw when pre-aimed (the only way under 100 ms); a heard enemy chased 100 to
  200 ms later than a seen one. On 4on4 e1m2 (three seeds) the drawn means came out 157, 220 and
  295 ms and the match's frags a minute rose from 6.9 to 9.6. Next: hit rates per weapon held to
  the division bands, prediction, grenade bounces, camp spots from the kill map.
- **M18r11d, hit rates per weapon** (`games/shooters_edge/net/classic_hits.hpp`): **measured,

  been read as shots. With that fixed, the trigger reading the view the command carries (a swim or
  a button had turned it off the aim), the arena's teleporters telefragging (players had stacked
  inside each other at a destination and shot each other unharmed for whole matches), a finger as
  loose as the aim trait, the double kept within 350 units and close rockets aimed at the middle,
  dm6 drills over two seeds read (novice / average / expert): the double 23 / 39 / 44 (bands 15–25
  / 25–35 / 35–45), the arc 24 / 36 / 44 (15–25 / 28–35 / 38–45), a rocket's direct hit 16 / 18 /
  19 (10–18 / 22–28 / 30–35), its blast 36 / 36 / 34 (12–15 / 15–20 / 18–25), grenades 30 / 32 /
  33 (2–5 / 5–10 / 8–15). Next: prediction (the rockets' direct hits by skill), grenades and
  rockets fired blind into rooms and spawns (the blast and the grenades' rates come down with the
  shots no one sees land), camp spots from the kill map; on e1m2, dm2 and dm3 the nav graph leaves
  key items out of reach.
- **M18r12, expert bots bunny hop like a pro** (`plugins/shooter/hops.hpp`, `pilot.hpp`):

  turn it can make, the side switched mid-air toward the route, the jump on landing, a wall on the

  hop the simple way (forward and the side key of the turn, steady hands: their speed kept on a

  Held by `tests/shooter_hops_test.cpp` (an ideal hopper reads chained, turning at the most with no

  Open: the human-handed hop loses speed (the hands' spring lags the turn); the simple hop has no

- **M18r13, where kills happen on a map, from many demos** (`client/demo_scan.hpp`, lab

  nearest where it first shows). The lab reads a folder of one map's recordings and draws every
  kill and flight over the wireframe as an added-up density, picks out the GOLD SHOTS (the same

  Measured on 300 recent ztndm3 games from the public game list (51 hours, read in 8 minutes on

  947 times in 270 of the 300 games; 7,311 rockets went off at the feet (rocket jumps).
  Next: the bots read `gold.tsv` (a spot to pre-aim, a lane to fire a rocket down blind) in the
  team bots' aim (M18r11).
- **M18r14, bots on an existing server**: a team of our bots joins a live classic server (an
  existing server running the common match mod, built beside the checkout and never in the tree)
  and plays a 4on4 against people, so the bots are tested where it matters and every match leaves
  data to learn from. One headless process runs a whole team of bots, each its own classic client
  over UDP on the wall clock; two processes can run the two teams of a bots-only match, neither
  knowing the other. Each bot knows only what its own client is sent and what its teammates say
  on `say_team`; the process describes its team's game (the score and its trend, each area, each
  member) from its bots' beliefs without feeding anything back into them. The design is in
  `docs/shooters-edge.md` § Playing together, "Bots on an existing server". In five steps, each
  its own pull request:
  - **M18r14a, the client's world** (`net/classic_mirror.hpp`): **done.** The fighter's world rebuilt from
    what its client was sent (its own player and stats, the other players' places and dead flags,
    the entities of the newest frame, the frame's sounds, the frags, the prints), so the fighter
    reads no server at all; `run_team_match` plays on it beside the server-fed run, and every
    fact the server-fed fighter used that a client is never sent (another player's health, an
    item outside the client's visibility set read as taken) is found and closed. Held by a test
    (both runs score, no team kill, the mirror equal to the server wherever the client is sent
    it) and a golden for the client-fed run. Landed: the mirror, `run_team_match`'s `client_fed`

  - **M18r14b, the bot team process** (`shooters_edge_bots`): **done.** N fighters on N clients over UDP

  - **M18r14c, the narrator** (`bots/narrator.hpp`): **first pass in**: the team's report
    (`bot_team_report`: the score, each bot's health, armour, mode, role, goal and place) every

  - **M18r14d, a real match replayed and read** (lab `se-live`): the match's video following the

  - **M18r14f, the bots' play read off live matches**: **in progress.** Each change found by
