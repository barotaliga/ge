No cloud ends in a cut. Over a classic map's night a scud crossing the moon ended in a line a pixel wide against the moon's glow, because round the sun and the moon the glow is seen as the disc is (far over the picture's white), so a thousandth of a cloud's glow shows, and three stops in the cloud pass were each a step of more than that:

- a layer's march stopped at a hundredth of the light and took the rest as thick, while a ray past a thin wisp marched on into clear air and kept its hundredth. Within 0.1 rad of the key light the march now goes on to a millionth, back to the hundredth by 0.3 rad, so the rest of the sky costs what it did;
- the layers behind a nearly opaque one were dropped at a hundredth; they now fade out with what is let through;
- a layer under a ten-thousandth of cover was skipped; now only one under a ten-millionth is.

A dense layer's edge is feathered as well (`clouds.wgsl`'s `feather`): the density's share across the edge is taken to the power ln τ / ln(1/0.3), so every layer deeper than 3.3 reaches an optical depth of one 0.3 of the way into its edge. Heaps' edges are softer and their smallest puffs fainter.
