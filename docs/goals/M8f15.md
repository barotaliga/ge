A classic map's sky used to carry one deck of small heaps at 1.5 to 2.3 km over the share of the sky its texture's cloud layer had (63% on the red-brown sky), standing still and the same over every map; at night every map had the same scud and broken deck. Now `pick_sky(colours, map)` deals the map's wind and its day's stack off its name and `night_over` deals the night's (`plugins/brush/app/bsp_sky.hpp`: `SkyLayer`, `add_layer`, `set_cover`). Each layer is a kind from `render/cloud_kinds.hpp` at its own height over the map's top, its cells' span dealt across 70 to 110% of the kind's band, its patches (islands) 0.3 to 0.8 and its warp 0.6 to 1.5 cells.

The warp is the engine's (`CloudLayer::warp`, the frame block's `thick.w`): the column's read is pushed by two more reads of the cloud noise a tile apart, at the tile's own scale so the drift's wrap stays seamless, mirrored on the CPU (`render/clouds.cpp`'s `warped`) for the shadow and the tests. It is 0 by default, so every other sky (Fall Line's) is unchanged.

The title's sky keeps its fractus and stratocumulus at 30% and 38% (`add_layer`). The pictures (from the shareware pack, on a private page) are https://claude.ai/artifact/5XzpCUFnmeEPZe1xvHwFME.
