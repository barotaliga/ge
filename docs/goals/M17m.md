Labs: `net-stress`, `net-chaos`, `net-fuzz`, `net-load`, `se-load`

M17m1 is built. `engine/net/stress.hpp` holds four pressure tests over the fake-traffic session:

- ramps that bisect each link knob to its breaking point;
- eight chaos scripts, with reconnects;
- a soak that holds memory flat;
- a fuzzer beside an honest session.

`ge net stress` runs them from the command line, and the `net-stress`, `net-chaos` and `net-fuzz`
labs chart them. The traffic plan gained the chaos steps, the reconnect tries, a per-tick hook and
summed latencies. A session without them keeps its digest, so `net-link`'s smoke digest is
unchanged.

Measured, against the done-when cell:

- **Breaking points.** `ge net stress` names each knob's breaking point for a mix. For racing with
  eight clients from broadband: 14% loss, bursts of 56 drops, 250 ms of latency or jitter, and
  54 kbit/s.
- **Soak.** An hour at 32 clients takes 12 s in the release build. Memory held flat at 34 MB. The
  packet sequence and the reliable ids each wrapped twice, with every reliable message delivered.
- **Fuzz.** A million fuzzed datagrams went through the parser and the node under the debug
  build's ASan and UBSan. The first run found one undefined shift: `place_sequence` shifted the
  ack bits by the sequence's jump, which a forged sequence or a long outage can push past 64. It
  is fixed, `tests/net_connection_test.cpp` holds it, and the rerun is clean. The honest session
  never noticed.
- **Chaos.** Every chaos script ends with each client reconnected or cleanly timed out.

M17m2:

- **Load: done.** `run_load` runs many full sessions in one process on one simulated network, each
  host's step timed by the caller's clock; the `net-load` lab (in `make labs-smoke`) and
  `ge net stress --load=<n>`. In the debug build with ASan, 32 sessions of 32 clients (1,056
  nodes) fit in 91% of a 60 Hz tick, a host's step about 0.48 ms however many share the process.
  It found the simulated network's socket lookup linear in the nodes (a host's step 1.26 ms at
  32 sessions); indexed, the step is flat and every other net lab's digest is unchanged.
- **Bots: done.** Shooter's Edge's `se-load` lab (in `make labs-smoke`) runs matches of its bots,
  each bot a classic-protocol client over the simulated network, on many servers for each count
  of players, and times the server's frame apart from the bots' thinking
  (`TeamMatchOptions::clock_ns`; `tests/shooters_edge_team_test.cpp` holds that timing changes
  nothing). Debug build with ASan, the fixture halls, two servers a size: the frame takes 1.0 ms
  at 2 players, 1.7 at 4, 3.5 at 8 and 7.9 at 16 (p99 13.8 ms), the wire 15 to 397 KB/s. A
  release build's figures are the one thing left to read off it.
- **Rebinding: done.** A sealed session keeps a connection when a NAT rebinds a client's port:
  the host takes the new address on the first payload that opens under that peer's key, inside
  its replay window, behind the strangers' guard (`rebind_peer`). The `net-chaos` lab's sealed
  rebind (`--secure=both`, in its smoke flags) ends with no drop; `tests/net_node_secure_test.cpp`
  holds that a stranger's payload from a new address moves nothing. A plain session still
  reconnects, since nothing in a plain payload proves its sender.
