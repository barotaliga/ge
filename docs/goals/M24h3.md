Done: the surface from below is the water pass's own underside (`water.below`, the same rings and
waves as from above): each pixel refracted out by Snell's law and weighed by the exact Fresnel terms
for light leaving water, the world above read along the bent ray, so the whole hemisphere is
squeezed into the window with the room round its rim, from any depth; total internal reflection
past it mirrors the water below. A game's own views (`WaterViews`: a planar mirror, the view up) feed
both; a classic map's are `brush/app/bsp_views.hpp`. The caustics are the real surface's (its
curvature's Jacobian along the sun's refracted way, blurred by the sun's disc with depth), on what
stands under the surface and on the bed seen from above; the light shafts are the same focusing
marched along the eye's ray and scattered forward. Both only where the sun reaches the water.
`gpu-underwater` names both views (`caustics`, `shafts`), each off beside on and held to add
contrast, and measures the window by ring means (49 degrees at 640 x 360).
