Status: in progress
Milestone: **Classic remix mode**: a feature of classic-enhance itself (its switch in classic-enhance's own menu, not a separate mode of the game), in which the player plays one classic game with parts of the others they own: the classic multiplayer game's maps with the sequel's weapons and items, its armour, its player models, or another game's maps under one game's rules and movement, each part chosen on its own. The mode is ONE switch, shown only when at least two classic games are loaded as FULL games (their registered packs; a shareware pack never counts, nor does a game with only its shareware pack); off by default, so a classic game loaded on its own uses only its own assets (never Shooter's Edge's, and never another game's)
Exit: The switch hidden with one full game, or with any number of shareware packs, and shown with two full games; a remix chosen in the menu and by cvars (each part's source game), refused at load with the missing pack named when a part cannot resolve; the classic multiplayer game's map played by bots with the sequel's weapons and items, and separately its armour and player models, each with a golden digest; the same map with remix off drawing and playing exactly as before (digests unchanged)
Labs: `se-remix`, `se-remix-physics`, `se-remix-sounds`

Builds on the classic game profiles (M18t): each game's adapter answers for its own files, and the
remix mode is the one place that asks two adapters at once. It needs the sequel's profile
(M18t3, M18t4), the remix check at load (M18t5) and the arsenal as data (M18t6: weapons and
armour as rows a map's item names map onto by slot). Every part goes through the games' adapters, the HUD and the main menu too (each game's own
HUD and menu pages, chosen like any other part), and a game's mods stay its own (each
game's adapter lists its own mod folders and finds its own programs; a remix takes one game's
mod as its rules), and both the multiplayer and the single-player games can be remixed, except the third game's, which has no single-player campaign to remix. Player
models and HUD pictures follow the same rule: a part is taken whole from one game, so a borrowed weapon brings its own models,
sounds and HUD icon from its own game's packs.

The sequel's profile has landed (M18v), and the maintainer asked for the first two parts
(2026-11-13): another game's maps, their items made the played game's, and another game's items
on a game's own maps with that game's HUD. The switch is the setting `cr_enabled` (0 by default),
which does nothing unless two classic games are loaded as full games; `cr_items` names whose items
a map is played with (0 the played game's, 1 the classic game's, 2 the sequel's). The parts and
their order:

- **M18u1, the item table and the settings**: **landed**: each game's map items as the other's,
  by role (`profiles/remix.hpp`: a weapon by its place in the set, an ammunition by its weapon,
  health and armour by size, a powerup by what it does; the variant bits replaced, the skill and
  mode bits kept, an item with no match left out and counted), the entity text rewritten
  (`client/remix.hpp`'s `remix_entities`), `cr_enabled` and `cr_items`, and the check that wants a
  second full game (`remix_check`, its refusal in the game's words). Lab `se-remix` draws a real
  map of each game from above with its own items and with the other game's.
- **M18u2, the sequel's maps in the classic game**: **landed**: a map found only in the other
  game's packs played under the classic game's rules, its items the classic game's: the classic
  rules tracing a version 38 map's models through their brushes (`sh::Server`'s model solids),
  the other game's packs under the played game's for the map and its wall textures
  (`RemixFiles`), its palette its own. The weapons are balanced against the rest of the map
  (`RemixBalance`: `cr_strong_min` strong weapons at least, `cr_strong_share` of them at most,
  the best weak one raised or a strong one lowered; `cr_strong_same` copies of one strong weapon at most, the copy nearest a strong armour lowered first), and the sequel's small health and
  armour shards are gathered by `cr_pool_radius` into the classic game's packs by the total they
  hold. Lab `se-remix` surveys fourteen real maps (their weapons by role, the balance's steps,
  the items with no match); `se-client --remix=<install> --map=<the other game's map>` draws one
  in the window's frame.
- **M18u3, the classic game's maps in the sequel**: **landed**: the sequel's rules on a version
  29 map, its point tree's cells made brushes (`brush/cells.hpp`: one brush a path to a non-empty
  leaf, the planes that bound nothing left out, axial bevels added) so the brush trace answers on
  it, its items the sequel's, its textures read as its own game stores them. Every rewritten item
  keeps the middle of its floor (`profiles::item_foot`: the games measure an item's place from
  different points of its box), so it no longer starts inside the floor and vanishes.
- **M18u4, the other game's items on a game's own maps**: `cr_items 2` on the classic game's maps
  (the sequel's arsenal rows into the classic rules: its weapons' models, sounds and pickups) and
  the sequel's HUD drawn over the classic stats; `cr_items 1` on the sequel's maps, the classic
  HUD over the sequel's. In parts:
  - **M18u4a, the classic rules in the sequel's weapons and armour**: **landed**: the classic
    rules keep their numbers and the map its class names, and wear the sequel's things from the
    next map on (`profiles/remix_arsenal.hpp`, by role as the item table: the nailguns the
    machinegun and chaingun, the beam the railgun): each weapon's view model in the hand (its
    idle frame the rest, its firing frames the shot), its pickup on the floor, its missile, its
    fire, bounce and blast sounds, and the three armours' models and pickup sounds; another
    game's model keeps its skin's own colours. `se-client --remix-items=1` draws it.
  - **M18u4b, the sequel's bar, boxes and pickup sounds**: **landed**: the sequel's own status
    bar program run over the classic status (`client/remix_bar.hpp`: health, the ammunition of
    the weapon in hand as the sequel's weapon holds it, the armour by class, the weapon just
    picked up, a power-up's seconds, the frags), its pictures from the remix's files; the classic
    rules' ammunition and health boxes in the sequel's models and pickup sounds and a weapon's
    pickup sound the sequel's (`arsenal_ammo<n>`, `arsenal_health<n>`, `arsenal_pickup`: the
    model about the box's middle, raised as far as that game's items stand over the floor).
  - **M18u4c, the sequel's rules in the classic game's things**: **landed**: rules that name
    their things in code wear the other game's by name: the sequel's server setting `sv_wear`
    (`cr_items 1` sets it to `classic` from the next map) renames each model and sound as the
    players are told it (`profiles/remix_arsenal.hpp`'s `kSequelAsClassic`: the guns by role,
    the armours by the classic armour's skin, the boxes and packs by kind and size, the missiles,
    the fire, blast and pickup sounds), and each item's row stands where the classic game stands
    it (on the floor; a box from its corner, never turning). The window colours a classic model
    through the classic palette (a brush model's textures put into the sequel's, glowing kept
    glowing), holds the classic view models in the sequel's weapon order and spreads the sequel
    hand's firing frames over each one's own (`held.cpp`). `se-client --game=<sequel>
    --remix=<classic> --remix-game=classic --remix-items=1` draws it.
  - **M18u4d, the classic bar over the sequel**: **landed**: while the sequel wears the classic
    game's things the window draws the classic status bar over its player
    (`client/remix_bar.hpp`'s `worn_status`: each weapon as the classic gun it wears, bullets in
    the nails' counter, grenades or rockets and slugs or cells by the gun in hand, the armour by
    class, the quad, invulnerability and suit seconds, the frags), its pictures read from the
    remix's files (`WornHud`).
- **M18u5, the players' figure**: **landed**: `cr_models` names the game whose figure every
  player is drawn as from the next map (0 each game's own, 1 the classic game's, 2 the
  sequel's; `profiles/remix_arsenal.hpp`'s `kRemixFigures`), read from the remix's files: the
  classic game's players as the sequel's marine in its own picture and colours with its weapon
  in hand, the sequel's as the classic game's figure (which stands alone, outside players/), its
  weapon in its own mesh so the one the sequel carries is not drawn; each figure's frames played
  from the other's by kind (`app/figure_frames.hpp`). `se-client --remix-models=1` draws it.
- **M18u6, the players' bodies' physics**: **landed**: `cr_physics` (0 each game's own, 1 the
  classic game's, 2 the sequel's; first named `cr_moves`) gives the played game's move the other
  game's numbers from the next map
  (`profiles/remix_arsenal.hpp`'s `kRemixPhysics`), each move keeping its own code. The classic
  rules' move is told the sequel's by server settings: a run of 300, friction 6, the air steered
  at 1 by the whole wish (`pm_airaccel`, `pm_airwish`) and the landing pause (`pm_landpause`: a
  landing faster than 200 down holds the next jump 0.144 s, 0.2 s past 400). The sequel's move
  takes the classic game's by `sv_physics classic`: a run of 320, friction 4, the strafing rule in
  the air in every mode, and no landing pause (`SequelMove`'s `max_speed`, `friction`,
  `land_pause`). Lab `se-remix-physics` runs and strafe-jumps each move on a floor under both: the
  classic move told the sequel's ends 4 s of hops at 389 u/s against the sequel's own 435, the
  sequel's told the classic game's at 655 against the classic game's 679.
  - **M18u6b, everything but the weapons**: **landed**: the switch covers the whole body, and
    nothing a weapon does (how shots and grenades fly, their damage and the push of a blast stay
    with the items). Swimming: the share of the wish a swimmer takes (`pm_swimscale`, 0.7 and
    the sequel's 0.5; `SequelMove::swim_scale`), the water's friction (`sv_waterfriction`, 4 and
    the sequel's 1) and the speed up a water jump gives (`pm_waterjump`, 310 and the sequel's
    350). A fall's harm: the classic arena hurts a hard landing as the sequel does under
    `sv_falldamage 1` (the speed down lost, squared x 0.0001, less in water; (that - 30) / 2 past
    30, the hard landing's sound, the classic fall's obituary), and the sequel under
    `sv_physics classic` hurts nobody for a fall. Gravity (800) and the step (18) are the same in
    both. Lab `se-remix-physics` swims each move in a pool too: the classic game's swimmer at
    187 u/s and the sequel's at 150, each told the other's at the other's speed.
  - **M18u6c, the stair jump**: **landed**: the sequel's move climbs a ledge a jump reaches still
    rising by a whole step in the air and keeps the rise, so a jump off it adds to that rise (the
    stair's or crate's double jump); the classic move climbs only by rising past the edge. The
    classic move takes the sequel's climb by `pm_airclimb 1` (a server setting of its own, told to
    clients in the server info), the sequel's drops it under `sv_physics classic`
    (`SequelMove::air_step`). The stock sequel has no dodge, so none carries over. Lab
    `se-remix-physics`'s stair table: feet over a 32 ledge after the best double jump, the classic
    game 44, told the sequel's 121; the sequel 118, told the classic game's 43.
- **M18u7, the balance's weak weapons and the sequel's bar in the labs**: **landed**: a remixed
  map keeps at most `cr_weak_same` (2) copies of one weak weapon (the shotguns' kind, after the
  strong ones are lowered into them), the last in the map laid down as the box of what it fires
  (`profiles/remix.hpp`'s `WeaponTier::ammo`; lab `se-remix`'s survey counts them: three of
  q2dm4's six shotguns become shells). The window's choice of bar is one function
  (`client/remix_bar.hpp`'s `draw_sequel_bars`: the sequel's own bar over its own things, its bar
  over the classic status, else the game's HUD), which the `se-client` lab now makes too, so a
  shot of the sequel on its own map shows the sequel's bar as the window does.
- **M18u8, a classic mod's rules on the other game's maps**: **landed**: a classic mod folder
  holding a compiled module (`qwprogs.qvm`) plays the window's match on that module
  (`client/game_folder.cpp` finds it, `ListenOptions::module`), on the classic game's maps and,
  under classic remix, on the sequel's: the module's server traces a version 38 map's models
  through its brushes as the bytecode's does (`server/qc_server.hpp`'s `map_models` and
  `brush_solids`). The mod sets the boundaries: with a module playing, `cr_items` and
  `cr_physics` are not applied (the mod's own weapons and physics), while the map's items are
  still the classic game's and the figures follow `cr_models`. Tested on a built tournament mod
  (its module kept outside the tree) on q2dm1: the player enters, the mod's welcome printed
  (`se-client --game=<mod folder> --remix=<the sequel's folder> --map=q2dm1 --say=join` notes
  what the server says); `tests/shooters_edge_listen_test.cpp` plays the test module.
- **M18u9, the sounds (`cr_sounds`)**: **landed**: `cr_sounds` (0 the played game's, 1 the
  classic game's, 2 the sequel's) names the game every multiplayer sound is heard as, by a table
  of matching sounds (`profiles/remix_sounds.hpp`'s `kSoundMatches`: each weapon's shot, the
  blasts, bounces and ricochets, the pickups, an item coming back, the teleport, a player's jump,
  landing, pain, death, gibbing and splash; the sequel's footsteps silent as the classic game's).
  The client hears by it (`client/demo_sound.hpp`'s `hear_as`), so it holds whoever's rules play,
  a mod's too. `cr_items` alone no longer changes a sound: the worn weapons fire with the played
  game's shots until `cr_sounds` names the other game. Names both games share (the powerups',
  `misc/talk.wav`) play the played game's file. The `se-remix-sounds` lab draws every pair's
  waveforms off the games' own packs and tables what the worn arsenal is heard as.
- **M18u10, over a mod (`cr_over_mods`)**: **landed**: a mod's own rules (a compiled module or a
  game library of the sequel's interface) keep their weapons and physics, but with `cr_over_mods
  1` the physics `cr_physics` names are set over the mod's: the window's server takes them after
  its own lines (`client/remix.hpp`'s `wear_lines`, `ListenOptions::over_mod`), and the engine's
  move the sequel's game library asks for runs the classic game's ground and air under
  `sv_physics classic` (`server/sequel_native_calls.cpp`'s `c_pmove`, `NativeHost::classic_physics`).
  Sounds (`cr_sounds`) and figures (`cr_models`) are the client's and hold over any mod already.
  What a mod's weapons do is its own code and never swaps; the HUD follows the weapons in use.
- **M18u11, monsters (`cr_monsters`)**: **landed** (Niclas: the other game's monster whole,
  with its own health, attacks, model and sounds, in the played game's campaign). Landed: the
  table pairing each game's campaign monsters by role, then strength (`profiles/remix_monsters.hpp`:
  rifleman and guard, hound and light guard, swordsman and berserker, the heaviest walkers, the
  swimmers; bosses are not rows and stay the map's), `cr_monsters 0/1/2` (the played game's, the
  classic game's, the sequel's; from the next map), and the map rewrite (`client/remix.hpp`'s
  `remix_monsters`): a monster becomes the chosen game's when the played game's rules run it
  (`kRunsMonsters`), else the played game's partner. The classic campaign runs the sequel's
  monsters of the table (`rules/campaign_sequel_kinds.cpp`): the roster's health, box, speeds and
  attacks (wind-up, shots on a loop, another loop at its odds, recovery), the sequel's model,
  frames (`server/sequel_monster_poses.hpp`) and sounds, over the campaign's minds. A medic
  turns from the hunt to a corpse it sees within 400 units, walks to it and raises it within 96
  (whole health, counted again, after the medic's enemy); a brain's power screen is armour that
  takes a third of each blow until it has taken 100. The window's server reads the other game's files while
  `cr_monsters` names it (`RemixFiles::from_files`); the client lab takes `--remix-monsters=1`.
  The sequel's rules run the classic game's monsters as rows of their roster
  (`server/sequel_monster_kinds.hpp`, the classic campaign's numbers; their frame runs in
  `server/sequel_monster_poses.hpp`), so `cr_monsters` swaps whole both ways; the classic spawn's death
  bursts on whoever is near (the roster's `burst`: 120 less half the distance) as it does at home.
- **M18u12 The menu and the letters**: `cr_menu` names the game whose menu pictures the window
  draws (the plaque, the titles, the strips or the plates, the cursor, the console's background)
  and `cr_charset` the game whose glyph sheet writes the menu's rows, the console and the status
  bar's counts, each apart from the other (`client/remix_pictures.hpp`, read from the remix's files
  through that game's adapter); the pages stay the played game's. The window now reads the played
  game's own pictures through its adapter, so the sequel's menu is its plates. Lab: `se-remix-menu`.
  The sequel's own status bar writes its words in `cr_charset`'s letters too (`bar_pictures`;
  `se-sequel-hud --letters`).
