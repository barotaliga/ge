Status: in progress
Milestone: **Classic remix mode**: a feature of classic-enhance itself (its switch in classic-enhance's own menu, not a separate mode of the game), in which the player plays one classic game with parts of the others they own: the classic multiplayer game's maps with Quake II's weapons and items, its armour, its player models, or another game's maps under one game's rules and movement, each part chosen on its own. The mode is ONE switch, shown only when at least two classic games are loaded as FULL games (their registered packs; a shareware pack never counts, nor does a game with only its shareware pack); off by default, so a classic game loaded on its own uses only its own assets (never Shooter's Edge's, and never another game's)
Exit: The switch hidden with one full game, or with any number of shareware packs, and shown with two full games; a remix chosen in the menu and by cvars (each part's source game), refused at load with the missing pack named when a part cannot resolve; the classic multiplayer game's map played by bots with Quake II's weapons and items, and separately its armour and player models, each with a golden digest; the same map with remix off drawing and playing exactly as before (digests unchanged)
Labs: `se-remix`, `se-remix-physics`, `se-remix-sounds`, `se-remix-ladder`, `se-remix-q1-arms`, `se-remix-items`, `se-remix-q2-hand`

Builds on Quake profiles (M18t): each game's adapter answers for its own files, and the
remix mode is the one place that asks two adapters at once. It needs Quake II's profile
(M18t3, M18t4), the remix check at load (M18t5) and the arsenal as data (M18t6: weapons and
armour as rows a map's item names map onto by slot). Every part goes through the games' adapters, the HUD and the main menu too (each game's own
HUD and menu pages, chosen like any other part), and a game's mods stay its own (each
game's adapter lists its own mod folders and finds its own programs; a remix takes one game's
mod as its rules), and both the multiplayer and the single-player games can be remixed, except the third game's, which has no single-player campaign to remix. Player
models and HUD pictures follow the same rule: a part is taken whole from one game, so a borrowed weapon brings its own models,
sounds and HUD icon from its own game's packs.
