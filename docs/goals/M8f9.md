Status: in progress

Every cloud layer stands at its true height over the valley floor. It is marched on the eye's rays, so it needs no world under it. Each layer drifts on the wind aloft at its own height, so a low one crosses the sky faster.

As dusk falls (`dusk_of`), the evening's low-level jet speeds up the wind a few hundred metres up (`jet_at`, to 1.7 times the day's at 400 m). From twilight, a fair, flurrying, grey or snowing sky in a wind also gets ragged scud (the `fractus` kind) racing under it at 200 to 450 m and across a lit moon. The scud is dealt on a salt of its own, so no other layer moves.

`mountain/app/cloud_field.hpp`'s `Aloft` carries dusk and a height and speed picked by hand. Fall Line's cvars for them are `sv_cloudheight` (the lowest layer's base over the floor, m, the layers over it in proportion) and `sv_cloudspeed` (a share of the true wind, which is now the game's own: the layers drifted at a quarter of it before, so a low cloud hung still). The `world` lab's `--at` shoots each named view at the sky's moments, for a sequence of the cloud on the wind.

Next: integrate the drift over a running sky clock (`sv_skyrate`), so the jet winding up never moves a layer on; and scud torn off a deck's base in rain (pannus) with its own darker look.
