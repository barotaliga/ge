Status: in progress
Milestone: **Sequel support in classic-enhance**: the classic game's sequel loaded from the player's own packs as its own game behind its adapter (its maps, models, sounds, HUD, main menu, weapons, mods, configs), its rules as a second rules runner on `engine/mod` and its own move, in multiplayer and single player; every file family read once by what it is (shared readers, shared adapter calls), each game only thin glue, so the games' files mix in remix mode
Exit: The sequel's demo pack loaded through its own adapter, a map drawn in the window and played by a bot under its own rules runner with a golden digest; the classic game's digests unchanged
Labs: `se-sequel`, `se-sequel-fight`, `se-sequel-coop`, `se-sequel-bots`

Gathers the sequel's steps of the classic game profiles (M18t): M18t2 (clean-room research
for its packs, maps, models, rules and move), M18t3 (its content drawn) and M18t4 (its rules
and move). It builds on the per-game adapter (`games/shooters_edge/client/game_adapter.hpp`) and
the rules runner (`docs/shooters-edge.md` § The rules runner). A loaded sequel uses only its own
assets; mixing it with another game is classic remix (M18u). The rule throughout: a file is read
by its kind, never by the game that holds it (`client/adapter_parts.hpp`), and what two games
would write alike is lifted into the shared layer rather than copied. THE GOAL IS REMIX: each
part (maps, collision, movement, weapons and armour, items, models, sounds, HUD, menus, rules,
bots) is a slot with one shape every game fills, so any game's part plays with any other's (the
sequel with the first game's move, the third's maps with the first game's weapons); each step's
lab shows its part working with another game's. The plan, step by step, is
the working page `porting/shooters-edge/sequel-plan.md`.

- **M18v1, the files open and a map is entered**: **landed**: the sequel's content row
  (`plugins/classicenhance/profile.hpp`, its palette the one at the end of a PCX picture:
  `palette_of`) and game row (its native rules named and passed over); the calls every classic
  game's adapter shares lifted into `client/adapter_parts.hpp` and the sequel's adapter on them
  (`client/sequel_adapter.cpp`); wall texture files (`plugins/brush/wal.hpp`), read for any map
  that keeps its pictures outside, in whichever game's folder; the world draw takes its palette
  from the content row; lab `se-sequel` enters each map from its start and draws it four ways.
- **M18v2, the window plays it**: **landed**: the install recognised by its folders
  (`client/game_folder.hpp`'s `install_of`: the first content row whose base folder and first pack
  it holds), at the start, from the game kept, and by LOAD GAME, which swaps the window's adapter;
  a map of the 38 family read for the server with its brushes (`server/bsp_map.hpp`), played on
  the sequel's runner whatever the program (`net/classic_game.cpp`), its world mirrored into the
  loop's pool the endpoint and the view read; a turn the spawn gives is taken once by a client
  that adopts the server's view (`SequelServerOptions::clients_take_view`); the bots sit out a map
  of brushes until M18v8. Lab `se-sequel` walks each map with brushes on the window's own server
  and draws the view where the player stopped.
- **M18v2c, one trace for both families**: the two families' collision (clip hulls, brushes)
  behind one trace, so every `sv_playermove` value and every program runs on every map (the remix
  slot for collision). **The trace landed (2026-11-12)**: `MoveWorld`'s solids gain a third kind,
  `brushes`, a version 38 map's model traced by the brush tracer, so `player_trace`, `box_free`,
  the contents and the new `point_trace` (sight, a rocket's burst, a grenade's toss) answer on
  either family (`tests/shooter_world_test.cpp`); open: the nav and the bots' world built from a
  38 map (M18v8's first step), the first game's move run on one.
- **Order (Niclas, 2026-11-11)**: after M18v2, our own clean-room game comes first (M18v7: the
  deathmatch rules, then the whole single player with every monster), then the rest.
- **M18v2b, coloured light in the modern look**: the 38 family's light is coloured (RGB
  lightmaps, light entities' colours, faces that emit light by their surface flag and value); the
  modern look carries that colour through (its real-time lights, the rebake, the bloom) instead of
  greying it, and the brightness rule (texture and lightmap scale) is settled, clean-room; lab
  `se-sequel` before and after on the demo's maps.
- **M18v3, models and their animation**: the second triangle-model family and its sprites into
  `plugins/morph`; the map's models standing; the player models and skins; the held weapons; and
  every model ANIMATED as the game animates it (a frame set each 100 ms server frame, drawn

  films each kind (items turning, players running, firing and dying, monsters, the held weapons)
  frame by frame and fails a frame that jumps or freezes.
- **M18v4, pictures and sounds**: the status bar by its layout language, the console font, its
  own menu pages, the sky box, the players' own sounds.
- **M18v5, files and mods**: the search order as a content-row field (packs over loose files), its
  mod folders, its default config.
- **M18v6, classic-enhance on it**: every stage on the content row, the same `ce_` switches.

- **M18v8, the bots lifted**: the bot whole (mind, goals, fighting, footwork, routes, team play,
  talk, map plan and navigation) behind a game-neutral seam (map knowledge from either map family,
  items and weapons from the rows, the move as knobs), each game's bots glue; started once the bot
  work now in flight is on `main`. **M18v8a, the map nav on either family (2026-11-12)**:
  `bots/map_nav` is built off what every rules runner keeps alike (`RulesCommon`), so the
  sequel's rules build it as the first game's do; on a version 38 map its world, its doors and its
  plats trace the map's brushes through one tracer the nav keeps (a mover's shape is a clip-hull
  model or a brush model, `NavMover::tracer`), the sequel's teleporter pads link to their
  destinations, and a brush entity whose row carries no box takes its model's
  (`tests/shooters_edge_sequel_nav_test.cpp`). **M18v8b, the arsenal (2026-11-12)**: a game's
  weapons as a bot sees them are one table, `bots/bot_arsenal` (a row a weapon: its bit, its
  impulse, the ammunition it spends, how it is aimed and fired, its curve of worth by range, its
  worth to pick up and the arms it lends; the game's ammunitions, carry limits and item table; the
  key weapons by part: launcher, lob, beam, heavy). The fighter and the rest of the bot name no
  weapon: `first_arsenal()` holds the first game's play bit-exact (the bots' lab digests are
  unchanged), `sequel_arsenal()` reads the sequel's ten weapons and six ammunitions off its rules'
  table, and the sequel's server sends a player's held and carried weapons and every ammunition
  (`PlayerNumbers::held`, `carried`, `stock`). **M18v8c, a sequel deathmatch (2026-11-12)**: the
  bots' sight and every trace a bot asks of the pool go through the map's brushes when the pool
  holds a version 38 map (`sh::Server::brushes`, set by the sequel's rules as they mirror their
  world); an item's place is its row's origin when the row carries no box. Lab `se-sequel-bots`:
  four bots, two minutes on a hall with a doorway, a plat to a ledge, a pad and five weapons; they
  frag, pick up and hold every weapon there, the railgun on the ledge too. **M18v8d, the movers
  traced (2026-11-12)**: a pool's rules may give it their own trace (`sh::Server::world_trace`);
  the sequel's gives `map_trace`, so a bot's sight and lines stop at doors and plats where they
  stand (`tests/shooters_edge_sequel_nav_test.cpp`; the lab's two minutes went from 7 frags and
  25 pickups to 9 and 31). Open: the nav and the pilot plan with the first game's move (the
  sequel's air control is weaker). **M18v8e, the companions (2026-11-12)**: in the sequel's
  single-player or co-op game `addbot [skill]` seats a co-op companion (`bots/companion.hpp`) on
  its own rules, the window's player its leader, and the game turns co-op while one is seated
  (`client/sequel_party.hpp`, `net/classic_game.cpp`'s `mate_words`); `coopgame [bots] [skill]
  [map]` seats them as the game starts (1 by default). They stay seated through a level change
  (the same rules), their nav rebuilt on each map; a new game or deathmatch sends them away. The
  companion reads the sequel's monsters off its roster (`BotArsenal::monster`) and takes a view
  the rules turned (a spawn, a teleport) as its own, so the turn is spent. Lab `se-sequel-coop`
  adds the companion's walk beside the player; test
  `a_companion_joins_the_sequel_s_single_player_game_and_follows_through_a_level_change`.
