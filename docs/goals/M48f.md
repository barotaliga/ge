Status: in progress
Milestone: **Shooter's Edge's weapons placed and fired as players expect**: centred under the crosshair by default as the classic arena games hold it (`r_viewmodel_center`, 0 the modern place to the right), the hands and arms optional (`r_viewmodel_hands`), the weapons sat lower so their tops stay under the picture's middle, and every shot firing the engine's muzzle fire (flame, smoke, sparks; render/muzzle.hpp, the same fire classic-enhance gives the classic weapons) scaled with the gun, in the window and in the labs' stage
Exit: every weapon pictured centred, to the right and without hands, and fired with the engine's flame and smoke in the yard and in the window's frame
Labs: `se-viewmodel`, `se-muzzle`

Placement, compared with how first-person shooters hold their weapons:

- Classic arena games hold the weapon in the MIDDLE, its muzzle on the picture's middle line a
  little under the crosshair, its back leaving the bottom edge; nothing of the hands shows.
- Modern shooters hold it in the lower RIGHT, the muzzle pointing at the crosshair from below, the
  top of the weapon well under the picture's middle and the stock leaving the bottom right corner.
  Players who move it at all move it further right and down, and often draw it with a narrower
  lens of its own (about 54 to 68 degrees) so a wide view does not shrink and stretch it.
- Both keep the weapon's top in the lower 40 percent of the picture. Ours sat higher and further
  out: the offsets are lowered and pulled in (`weapons.cpp`), and centred is the default.

The fire: `games/shooters_edge/app/muzzle.hpp` turns each weapon's table row (a render/muzzle.hpp
preset and its knobs, at the true size, tuned like classic-enhance's: a real gun's few
milliseconds of glowing gas, the smoke what is left) into a look scaled by `kBuiltScale`, and
steps each shot per frame: the flame on this frame's muzzle (`HandFrame::muzzle_at`, riding the
kick and the `fire` clip), the puff left in the world, the sparks into the particles. The window
draws them in the engine's explosions pass (`client/world_draw.cpp`); `ce_muzzles 0` leaves the
light alone. The labs' CPU stage draws a flame of the same shape (a white-hot core, a tongue
cooling to red, a star of prongs) in place of the old cross of quads.
