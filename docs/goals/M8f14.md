Ten kinds of moon from real moons of other planets (`render/moons.hpp`): Phobos, Deimos, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Titan, Enceladus, Miranda and Triton. Each has its mean radius and semi-axes, its orbit's height over its planet's surface (so each is as large as it looks from its own planet: Io a little larger than the Moon, Phobos a third of it, Deimos about 2' across), its geometric albedo and colour, and its sidereal period, inclination and eccentricity.

`FrameView::moons` is a set of up to four of them, with or without the Earth's moon. `render/night.hpp`'s `set_moons` places the set at a julian day. Each moon goes round on its own circle near the celestial equator at its own period: Phobos is faster than the sky turns, so it rises in the west, and Triton goes round backwards. Each is locked face-on to its planet and rocks by its optical libration, unless a set spins it by hand. The real sun lights it, so two moons show two phases. A moon's light outside the air is its albedo times its solid angle's share of the sun's times the phase law, which is the same rule that gives the Earth's moon its 0.317 lx. A set without the Earth's moon hides that disc, sums the others' light and keys the night on it from the brightest one's direction.

`render/wgsl/moons.wgsl` draws each moon. A round kind is an ellipsoid hit in closed form; a dented one (Phobos, with its great crater, Deimos and Miranda) is marched inside that bound. Each face is the real moon's map (`render/moon_maps.hpp`): the public-domain global mosaics from Viking, Voyager, Galileo and Cassini (USGS Astrogeology, NASA/JPL-Caltech; `render/moon_maps/SOURCES.md`), box-filtered to 1024 x 512 with their unseen hemispheres filled by the ground beside them reflected, scaled to a mean of 100, and for Io, Ganymede and Triton a 256 x 128 colour map. They are compiled in and uploaded as one mipmapped R8 array and one RGBA8 array, a layer a kind, and sampled in the body frame at the mip level the moon's size on screen asks. Titan keeps a fifth of its near-infrared map's contrast under its haze shell. The light is Lommel-Seeliger's blended toward Lambert's and is drawn as seen. A moon smaller than a pixel and a half is drawn at that size with its light shared over it. Post's night vision leaves each moon's disc to the cones.

Each sky type has its own set (`SkyType::moons`): the Earth has its moon alone, the dust has Phobos and Deimos, the alien sky has Io, Europa and Ganymede, and so on. Fall Line's `sv_moons` picks a set by name (`type`, `none`, `phobos deimos`, `moon io:2:5`). The `world` lab has a `moons` view and `moon1`..`moon4` close-ups. The Earth's own sky is unchanged: a set with only the Earth's moon places nothing, and the frame block's new rows stay zero.

Next: the night side lit by its planet (planetshine), one moon's shadow and eclipse on another, a parallax for the close ones (Phobos is never seen from near the poles), and the classic maps' night (M8f10) given a set.
