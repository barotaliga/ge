Milestone: **Classic maps at night**: every classic BSP map under an evening or a night of its own (dealt off its name; never day or morning) with a moon up, always windy and partly cloudy with low scud racing across the moon under a broken deck; the eye kept adapted to the rooms and the night sky lifted against them through the sky faces; the wind aloft and the jet moved to `engine/wind/aloft`

Labs: `gpu-bsp`

Every classic map is seen in the evening or at night (`plugins/brush/app/bsp_sky.hpp`'s `night_over`, on by default under v2). Each map's hour is dealt off its name: an evening (the sun 3 to 6 degrees down, a full moon rising opposite the afterglow) or a night (the moon 25 to 50 degrees up and more than half lit). It is always windy (8 to 14 m/s) and always partly cloudy with low cloud: scud (`fractus`) a few hundred metres over the map's top under a broken deck of rolls (`stratocumulus`). Both drift on the wind aloft at their heights and on the evening's low-level jet, which moved to `engine/wind/aloft.hpp` so the mountain and the brush plugin read one copy. The sky type and tint picked from the map's sky texture are kept.

The eye stays adapted to a torch-lit hall's light (`SkyPick::adapt_cd`, 5 cd/m^2), so the rooms and the lava keep their look. The night sky through the sky faces is lifted against them (`Options::sky_seen`, the veil now a factor the fragment writes, so it can lift as well as dim), up to 40 times, less in the evening's twilight.

The `gpu-bsp` lab: `--night=0` keeps the old daytime pick, `--at` shoots each view at the sky's moments as a sequence, `--to-moon` turns that sequence to the moon, `--adapt` and `--seen` override the night's exposure.

Next: pick each map's best view of its moon for the lab; let a game's cvars pick the hour and the wind (`sv_` rows like Fall Line's); a sky type's own glow (a nebula's) dimmed at night so the moon leads.
