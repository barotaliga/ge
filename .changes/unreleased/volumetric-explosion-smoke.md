type: Changed
title: Explosions' smoke shades itself, so a blast reads as a volume
---

An explosion's smoke is now lit through the smoke between each point and its light: the ball's chord toward the sun (or toward the lamps and sky above indoors) and two reads of its billows, the sky's light through the chord above, and the fire's glow through one read toward the core. Its underside and far side sit in their own shade, the lobes shade each other and the fire glows out through the thin parts, where the smoke used to draw as an evenly lit grey disc. The fire gets darker lanes of soot, and the gas's edge is crisper.
