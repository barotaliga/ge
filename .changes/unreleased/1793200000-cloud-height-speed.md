Clouds drift on the wind at their own height, so low ones cross the sky faster. As dusk falls, the wind a few hundred metres up speeds up (the evening's low-level jet), and from twilight ragged scud races low under a fair, flurrying, grey or snowing sky and across a lit moon. Fall Line's `sv_cloudheight` sets the lowest layer's base over the valley floor in metres, and the layers above it move in proportion. Clouds now drift at the true wind, four times faster than before. `sv_cloudspeed` sets how fast they drift, where 1 is the true wind and 0.25 the old speed. The `world` lab's `--at` shoots each view at several of the sky's moments, as a sequence.
