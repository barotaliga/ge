title: The thrown body's blows: every point of a falling skier is judged where it lands
---

A skier thrown off his skis is now hurt by what his body meets as it tumbles. Every point of it (the head, the shoulders, the hips, the knees, the feet, the elbows and the hands) that arrives in the snow or against a trunk is a blow. The snow gives more where it is loose than where it is groomed. Each blow reaches the parts beside it: the head's whips the neck, and loads it down the spine in a dive; a hand's runs up the arm; a shoulder's or a hip's reaches the trunk on the side he lands on. A landing the legs fold under now hurts the spine and the organs when his trunk reaches the snow, not when the skis touch. The `injuries` lab's head-on trunk curve now skis him into the trunk and judges the body it throws.
