title: The light shafts under a pool cut in two cost what one pool's do, and less looking across
---

Water tops at one level (a pool its bridge cuts in two, as dm3's) now count as one surface under water, so their light shafts take the one-surface march that reads each wave once for eight steps. That march also skips the waves its blur smooths away at every one of the eight, as the caustics already did. The shafts look the same, pixel for pixel. The `gpu-underwater` lab times the shafts looking across the bed too (`cost.across.*`).
