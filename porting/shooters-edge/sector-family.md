# Sector-based sprite shooter: clean-room facts

This file holds clean-room facts about the sector-based sprite shooter, read from the GPL source release, for docs/porting-sprite-games.md.

Sources read: id Software's DOOM source release (GPL-2.0), the Linux port at version 1.10, and for the last section the sequel's version 1.7 source (a French-language build). Every fact below is marked read (stated by the source), derived (arithmetic on read values) or measured (observed by running something; nothing was run, so no fact here carries that mark). Units: a unit is one map unit, a tic is 1/35 s, per-second figures are per-tic figures times 35.

## Confirmations of the known facts

All of these are read and correct: 35 tics per second; 16.16 fixed point; player radius 16, height 56, view height 41 above the feet; a step-up limit of 24; a use range and a melee range of 64; a bullet range of 2048; a map block of 128 units; health cap 100 (200 for the bonus items); ammo caps 200 bullets, 50 shells, 300 cells, 50 rockets (a backpack doubles them). Backpack pickup doubles the four ammo caps. [read]

## 1. The player's move

- Input per tic is a command of two signed bytes (forward, side) plus a turn. The keyboard produces forward 25 walking and 50 running, side 24 walking and 40 running, and a turn of 640 walking, 1280 running, 320 for the first 6 tics a turn key is held (a slow start). [read]
- Each command unit adds 1/32 unit per tic of momentum along the facing (forward) or 90 degrees to the right of it (side), only while the feet are on the floor. So thrust per tic is 0.78125 (walk forward), 1.5625 (run forward), 0.75 (walk side), 1.25 (run side). [read, derived]
- Turning adds the turn value times 65536 to a 32-bit angle: 640 is 3.52 degrees per tic (123 degrees per second), 1280 is 246 degrees per second, 320 is 61.5 degrees per second. Turning is not momentum. [derived]
- Order within a tic: the thrust is added, then the body moves by its whole momentum, then friction multiplies momentum by 0.90625 (59392 of 65536). [read]
- Resulting speed: the distance covered per tic settles at thrust divided by 0.09375, that is 8.33 units per tic (292 per second) forward walking, 16.67 (583 per second) forward running, 8.0 (280) side walking, 13.33 (467) side running. Pressing forward and side together adds the two vectors, so a running diagonal is about 21.3 units per tic (747 per second); the speed is not normalised. About 90 percent of the top speed is reached after 23 tics. [derived]
- Stopping: when no movement key is held and both momentum components are under 1/16 unit per tic, momentum is set to zero; otherwise friction applies. Friction does not depend on the floor, and it is skipped while airborne, for missiles and for flying lost souls. Each momentum component is also clamped to 30 units per tic (1050 per second). A move longer than 15 units per tic is done in two halves. [read]
- Gravity: 1 unit per tic of downward speed added each tic (1225 units per second squared), doubled on the first tic of a fall; there is no terminal speed and no fall damage. Landing at a downward speed above 8 units per tic dips the view and plays a grunt sound; nothing else. There is no jump. In the air the player cannot thrust (no air control) and keeps momentum. [read]
- Standing on a step makes the view settle smoothly: the view height drops by the step size and recovers by a growing amount per tic. View bob is half of a bob value (speed squared over 4, capped at 16) times a sine of period 20 tics, so at most 8 units; it only happens on the ground. [read]
- Wall sliding: when a move is blocked the player tries to slide. It traces the momentum from three corners of its box, finds the nearest blocking line (a one-sided line it faces, or a two-sided line it cannot fit through or step over), moves up to a hair (1/32 unit) short of it, then projects the unspent fraction of the move onto the line direction, scaling by the cosine of the angle between them, and tries that move; momentum is replaced by the projected vector, so a glancing hit keeps most speed and a head-on hit keeps none. A failed slide is retried at most twice more, then the move is split into trying the y part and then the x part alone. Axis-aligned lines zero the perpendicular component exactly. Hitting a corner (no line found) uses the split. [read]

## 2. Actor blocking, steps and drop-offs

- Actors block each other regardless of height. A solid or shootable thing blocks if the squares around the two origins overlap (the test is square, not round: the two centres must differ by less than the sum of radii on both axes). There is no height test, so nothing can be stood on or walked under another actor. Pickups are the exception: an item is taken only when its base is between 8 units below the toucher's feet and the toucher's head. [read]
- Height is checked in these cases only: a missile flies over or under a thing it does not overlap vertically; bullets and aiming use the target's vertical extent; pickups (above); and the fit under a ceiling and over a floor (below). A missile does not damage things of its shooter's own kind (the baron and hell knight count as one kind) and explodes on them without damage, except that players can hurt each other. [read]
- Step-up: a move is refused when the highest floor under the new position is more than 24 above the feet. Ceiling: refused when the gap between the new floor and ceiling is less than the actor's height, and refused when the actor would have to lower itself to fit (ceiling above the feet by less than the height). Teleporting things skip the step and head checks. [read]
- Drop-off: unless the actor has the drop-off flag or floats, a move is refused when the floor under the new position is more than 24 above the lowest floor of any two-sided line the actor's box touches. Players, corpses and missiles carry the drop-off flag, so only live monsters are kept from ledges. Monsters do not plan around ledges; they simply fail the move and pick another direction. [read]
- Floating monsters that are blocked by height move 4 units per tic up or down towards the needed height instead. Monsters can open doors (plain and key-less manual kinds) by walking into them. [read]
- Edge case: the blocking search looks only 32 units beyond an actor's box when it checks other things, so an actor with a radius over 32 (the spider mastermind at 128, the arachnotron at 64, the mancubus at 48) is not reliably blocked by or detected at its edges. The source says so in a comment. [read]
- A thing that no longer fits under a moving floor or ceiling is turned to gibs if it is a corpse (and stops blocking), removed if it is a dropped item, ignored if it is not shootable, and damaged only by a crushing mover (see 4). [read]

## 3. Auto-aim

- Aim is a search along three zero-width rays from the shooter's centre: straight ahead, then 5.625 degrees (1/64 of a turn) to the left, then 5.625 degrees to the right; the first ray that finds a target wins. Range is 1024 units. The player's aim has no vertical input; the vertical window is plus or minus 0.625 slope (about 32 degrees) from a point 8 units above the shooter's mid-height. [read, derived]
- Along a ray, every two-sided line crossed narrows the window to what its floor and ceiling steps leave open; a one-sided line, or a window that closes, ends the search. The first shootable thing (not the shooter) whose vertical extent overlaps the remaining window is the target, and the slope used is the middle of the part of the target inside the window. [read]
- With no target the slope is zero, so shots fly level (hitscan) and missiles go straight at the facing angle. [read]
- Hitscan shots then fly from the same height at 2048 range. Pistol and chaingun: the first shot of a press is exact; later shots while held scatter by up to plus or minus 5.6 degrees (a triangular spread). Shotgun: 7 pellets each with that spread. Double barrel: see 14. [read]
- Missiles (rocket 20 units per tic, plasma 25, BFG 25) spawn 32 units above the feet and use the same search for their vertical slope. [read]

## 4. Line specials, movers and triggers

Specials are numbers on a line; a line with a tag acts on every sector with the same tag. All speeds are per tic.

**Trigger classes** [read]
- Walk over, once (the special is erased the moment the line is crossed, even if nothing could move): 2-6, 8, 10, 12, 13, 16, 17, 19, 22, 25, 30, 35-40, 44, 52-54, 56-59, 100, 104, 108-110, 119, 121, 124, 125, 130, 141.
- Walk over, repeatable: 72-77, 79-84, 86-98, 105-107, 120, 126, 128, 129.
- Use, once (a switch that changes picture; it is erased only when the command started something): 7, 9, 11, 14, 15, 18, 20, 21, 23, 29, 41, 49-51, 55, 71, 101-103, 111-113, 122, 127, 131, 133, 135, 137, 140.
- Use, repeatable (the switch returns after 35 tics): 42, 43, 45, 60-70, 99, 114-116, 123, 132, 134, 136, 138, 139.
- Manual doors (the player uses the door itself): 1, 26-28 raise and wait then close; 31-34 and 118 open and stay (once); 117 blazing raise.
- Shoot: 24 (once), 46 (repeatable), 47 (once).
- Only the front side of a line can be used. A use reaches 64 units. Non-players trigger by walking only teleports 39, 97, 125, 126 and the door/lift lines 4, 10, 88; missiles never trigger lines; monsters use only manual doors 1, 31-34 and not lines marked secret.

**Doors** [read]: normal speed 2 (70 per second), blazing 8; they stop 4 units under the lowest neighbouring ceiling; wait at the top 150 tics (4.3 s). Raise doors then close; open kinds stay; close kinds shut; "close then open after 30 seconds" waits 1050 tics shut. A door that meets something while closing reopens, except the plain close kinds, which keep pressing. Using a door that is moving down reverses it; using a waiting or rising raise door closes it at once; monsters never close doors. Key doors need the blue, yellow or red card or skull; a player without it hears a grunt and reads "You need a blue key to open this door" (for a locked switch line: "...to activate this object"; red and yellow likewise). A sector with special 10 shuts its door 30 s after the level begins; special 14 raises it after 5 minutes (10500 tics).

**Floors** [read]: speed 1 (35 per second), turbo 4. Targets: lowest neighbouring floor (lower); next higher neighbouring floor (raise to nearest); the lowest neighbouring ceiling capped by the sector's own ceiling (raise; the crushing kind stops 8 under it); plus 24; plus 512; plus the height of the shortest lower picture on the sector's lines; turbo lower stops 8 above the lowest neighbour. Some kinds copy the floor picture and sector special of a neighbour when done. A sound plays every 8 tics. Stairs: step height 8 at 0.25 per tic (slow) or 16 at 4 per tic (turbo); each following sector, found through adjacent same-picture sectors, rises one step more than the last, so they start together and arrive in turn. Donut (special 9): the tagged inner sector sinks, and the ring around it rises, both at 0.5 per tic, to the floor height of the sector outside the ring; the ring's picture and special are copied from that outer sector when it arrives.

**Lifts** [read]: down-wait-up-stay speed 4 (blazing 8), wait 105 tics (3 s), lowest point is the lowest neighbouring floor; perpetual raise speed 1, starts in a random direction, waits 105 tics at each end; raise to the next higher floor and change picture at 0.5; raise by 24 or 32 at 0.5. A stop line freezes a perpetual lift and a later trigger restarts it.

**Ceilings** [read]: speed 1, fast crusher 2; crusher floor is 8 above the floor; a crusher meeting a living thing slows to 1/8 per tic (the fast one does not). A crushing mover does 10 damage every fourth tic to a thing it cannot fit over, with a blood spray; corpses are gibbed. A stop line freezes crushers and a trigger restarts them. A non-crushing ceiling that is lowering stops against an obstacle and keeps trying; only doors reverse.

**Others** [read]: light turn on (12: to the brightest neighbour, 13: 255, 35: 35, 79-81 repeatable versions, 138 and 139 switches); 104 turns tagged lights to the dimmest neighbour; 17 starts a slow strobe. Teleport (39, 97, 125, 126): the destination is the map thing numbered 14 inside a sector with the line's tag; works only crossing from the front side; missiles never; the player is frozen for 18 tics; the arrival angle is the marker's; momentum is zeroed; a fog flash with sound appears at both ends; a player landing on a shootable thing kills it, and a monster teleporting is refused instead, except on map 30. Exits: 11 (use, normal), 51 (use, secret), 52 (walk, normal), 124 (walk, secret); sector special 11 also exits (see 5). Scrolling: only special 48, a wall that scrolls left 1 unit per tic. Wall and floor pictures animate globally, each animation advancing one frame per 8 tics for walls and flats alike (the speeds are in the animation list). An optional deathmatch time limit (command-line) ends the level when it counts down.

**Two specials on one sector** [read]: doors, floors, ceilings and lifts each keep one mover slot per sector. A new command is skipped for a sector whose slot is full, so a door cannot start on a sector with a floor mover, and so on. Crusher and lift lists are kept separately and are restarted by the stop-line logic. Lights are not tracked: any light command acts at once on a sector already strobing or glowing. Walk-over lines are consumed even when skipped (above).

## 5. Sector specials

[read] Light kinds: 1 random flicker between own light and the dimmest neighbour (dark phase 1 to 8 tics, bright phase either 1 or 65 tics because of a mask rather than a modulus); 2 and 3 strobe fast and slow (bright 5 tics, dark 15 or 35), starting at a random phase of 1 to 8 tics; 12 and 13 the same in sync; 4 the fast strobe that also hurts; 8 glow: light moves 8 per tic between the dimmest neighbour and its own light; 17 fire flicker: every 4 tics the light is set to its maximum minus 0, 16, 32 or 48, not below the dimmest neighbour plus 16. If no neighbour is dimmer the strobe's dark level is 0.
Damage (only to the player, only while the feet are on the floor, checked every 32 tics on the level clock): 5 does 10, 7 does 5, 16 and 4 do 20; the radiation suit stops all but, for 16 and 4, a 5-in-256 chance per check of full damage. 11 does 20 every 32 tics, switches the god-mode cheat off each time, cannot kill (a hit that would kill leaves 1 health) and ends the level when health reaches 10 or less. 9 is a secret: counted when the player enters it with the feet down, then cleared. An unknown sector special is a fatal error. Monsters take no sector damage. [read]

## 6. Sight and hearing

- REJECT: a bit table of sector against sector, row for the looker, bit set meaning the two can never see each other; set bits end the sight test with no geometry. It only affects sight (monster seeing, and attack decisions), not sound, movement or bullets. Unset bits go on to a real test from the looker's eye (three quarters of its height) to any part of the target using the map tree, narrowing a vertical slope window at each two-sided line. There is no distance limit. [read]
- Hearing: only the player firing a weapon (any, fist included) makes noise. The noise floods outwards from the shooter's sector through two-sided lines whose opening is above zero (closed doors stop it), marking each sector with the noise maker. The blocks-sound line flag allows the noise through once; a second flagged line along one path stops it. A sector reached with fewer blocks is revisited. [read]
- A monster looking for a player each tic takes the noise maker of its sector as its target if it is shootable; monsters marked ambush (deaf) ignore the noise and wake only if they can see the target. Otherwise a monster wakes when it sees a player: it checks players in turn (at most two per tic), ignores dead ones, needs sight, and only if the player is within 90 degrees of its facing (or within melee range). Being damaged also gives it a target. [read]
- Waking plays the see-sound, picked among variants by the gameplay random stream, at full volume for the two big bosses. [read]

## 7. Randomness

[read] One fixed table of 256 byte values, indexed by a counter that is incremented before each read, so the first value returned is the second entry (the table begins 0, 8, 109, 220, 222, 241). There are two counters over the same table: the gameplay stream and a second stream for everything cosmetic. Both are set to zero when a new game or a demo starts, not at each level; a saved game does not store the counters.
Gameplay stream: damage rolls, weapon spread, monster decisions (when to attack, chase direction timing, pain), see-sound variants, puffs and blood, the random start tic of each placed thing (one draw per thing with a duration, in map thing order), light flicker, strobe phase, a perpetual lift's start direction, crush blood, respawns, damage from the strobe slime with a suit.
Second stream: the screen melt, sound pitch variation (plus or minus 16, plus or minus 8 for chainsaw sounds, none for pickup and tink sounds), the status-bar face, intermission animation timing. Because sound uses it, hearing differences cannot desync a demo. In net games the consistency check uses the second stream's counter when the player has no body.

## 8. Demo files

[read] A demo is 13 header bytes then 4 bytes per active player per tic, then one end byte 0x80. Header: version number (110 in the version read; 107 in the sequel's source), skill (0 to 4), episode, map, deathmatch flag, respawn flag, fast flag, no-monsters flag, the console player, then four flags for which players are present. Per-tic bytes: forward (signed), side (signed), turn high byte (unsigned; the recorded turn is the command's turn rounded to a multiple of 256, about 1.41 degrees, and recording replays its own rounded value so live play matches), buttons.
Buttons: bit 0 attack, bit 1 use, bit 2 weapon change (weapon number in bits 3-5), bit 7 a game event (low two bits: 1 pause, 2 save game with the slot in bits 2-4).
To stay in sync playback needs: the same data files (maps, thing tables, behaviour tables), the same version number, the initial skill, the flags in the header, both random streams reset at start, the same thing order in the map file, the same fixed-point integer arithmetic and the same order of per-tic updates (players in order, then the running things in creation order). Mismatched version: the older source stops with an error, version 1.10 prints "Demo is from a different game version!" and skips the demo. A recorded demo is capped in size (128 KiB unless the maximum is raised on the command line).

## 9. Network play in the original

[read] Lockstep, peer to peer: every computer sends its commands for each tic to all others and the game tic advances only when the commands of every player for it have arrived; the slowest machine paces the game. Up to 4 players over up to 8 nodes. Each packet: a 4-byte word (checksum in the low 28 bits; flags in the top four: exit, retransmit request, setup, kill), then four bytes (retransmit start, start tic, player, number of tics), then that many commands of 8 bytes: forward (signed), side (signed), turn (16 bits), consistency (16 bits), chat character, buttons. Twelve tics are buffered. An option duplicates each tic 2 to 5 times for slow links, and another sends a backup of the previous tic in every packet. Lost packets are asked for again from the first missing tic, and answered by resending (10 resends per request). The faster players slow slightly when they run ahead.
Consistency: every tic each machine adds to its command the x position of its player (or the second stream's counter when it has no body); a mismatch on a later tic is a fatal error "consistency failure". A machine gives up waiting after about 20 tics and returns control to the menu. A turbo flag message ("is turbo!") appears if a player's forward command exceeds 50. [read]

## 10. Rendering rules a 3D conversion must keep

- Pegging, one-sided wall: the picture's top meets the ceiling (default) or its bottom meets the floor (lower-unpegged). Two-sided upper wall: default aligns the picture's bottom edge to the opening's top; upper-unpegged aligns its top to the higher ceiling. Two-sided lower wall: default aligns the picture's top to the opening's bottom; lower-unpegged keeps it fixed to the high ceiling's height as if it hung from it. The side's vertical offset always adds; the horizontal offset plus the position along the line gives the column. A wall column repeats vertically every 128 rows. [read]
- Masked middle picture on two-sided lines: drawn once with no vertical repeat, between the larger floor and the smaller ceiling; default anchors its top to the lower of the two ceilings, lower-unpegged anchors its bottom to the higher floor. Transparent gaps in the picture show what is behind. These are drawn after walls and sprites at their distance. [read]
- Sky: a ceiling (or floor) using the sky flat shows the sky picture instead of a surface; the sky is fixed to the viewing angle, 1024 columns around a full turn (so a 256-wide picture repeats four times around), is anchored with row 100 at the view centre line, ignores light and is not changed by the invulnerability colours. Where both sides of a two-sided line have sky ceilings the upper wall is never drawn, so ceilings of different heights open to the sky seamlessly. Missiles that strike a sky ceiling line vanish instead of exploding; sky floors are not handled. [read]
- Light: sector light 0-255 gives 16 bands (light divided by 16), plus 1 or 2 during a muzzle flash, minus 1 on walls running exactly east-west and plus 1 on walls running exactly north-south. The colour map is chosen from 32 (0 brightest) as four times the dark bands (15 minus band) minus roughly 1280 divided by distance in map units for the full-width 320-column view, clamped to 0..31. Sprites use the light of their sector and the same distance rule; frames flagged full-bright use map 0; the invulnerability effect is a fixed map (index 32) over everything but the sky; the light-amplification effect is the near-full-bright map 1. [read, derived]
- Fuzz (spectre and invisibility): the sprite's shape is drawn by copying each pixel from one row above or below the true one, following a repeating 50-entry up/down pattern advanced per pixel drawn, then darkening with colour map 6. The player's own weapon is drawn this way under invisibility; the effect blinks off during the last 128 tics. [read]
- Sprite facing: eight views chosen by the angle from the thing's facing to the viewer, rotated by half a step so that frame digit 1 is the view from in front; mirrored lumps share views. Things nearer than 4 units are not drawn. [read]

## 11. Sound and music

- Sound effects are lumps named by a letter pair then the effect name: "ds" plus name for digital samples, "dp" plus name for the PC speaker; a missing sample falls back to the pistol sound. The player's loader skips 8 bytes of header, then reads unsigned 8-bit samples centred on 128; a sample is padded with 128 to a multiple of 512. The header's fields (format, rate, count) are not decoded by this release, which plays everything at 11025 Hz, so the rate field and the 22050 case are unanswerable from the source. [read]
- Mixing in the Linux port: 8 channels, 11025 Hz, 8-bit; volume steps 0-15 in the menu, 0-127 inside; stereo separation swings 96 of 256 by the source's angle; volume is full within 160 units, falls linearly to zero at 1200 units (sounds beyond are dropped, except on map 8 where the distance never silences them and a floor of 15 is kept). Chainsaw and a few other sounds play one at a time. [read]
- Music: lumps are named "d_" plus the song name and passed whole, unparsed, to a third-party library (the sequel source calls it through register, play, chain-for-looping, pause and volume calls). The Linux release has no music code, so the header fields of the music format are unanswerable from the source. An instrument bank lump named "genmidi" is handed to the FM driver to define its voices; lumps "dmxgus" and "dmxgusc" (the sequel) map patches for the Gravis card. [read]

## 12. The held weapon

- Position is in a virtual 320 by 200 screen. The weapon rests with its x at 1 and y at 32, so the picture's own offsets place it; lowered it sits at y 128 (off the bottom). Lowering or raising moves 6 per tic, about 16 tics. [read]
- Bob: x equals 1 plus bob times cosine, y equals 32 plus bob times sine, with the angle advancing 1/64 of a turn per tic; the sine uses half a turn so y only ever moves down (a 32-tic cycle), x has a 64-tic cycle. Bob is the player's speed squared over 4 in units, capped at 16; zero standing still, 16 at ordinary walking speed. It runs only while the weapon is ready, not firing. [read]
- Frame timing: a state's action runs when the state is entered; the tics value is how long the frame then stays. Held-fire cycle (tics between shots, shot at the start): fist 17, pistol 14, shotgun 37, double barrel 57, chaingun 4 (two shots per two states), rocket launcher 20 (needs a new press), plasma 3, BFG 60 (new press; shot 30 tics after the press), chainsaw 4. Letting go adds a dwell back to ready (pistol 5, plasma 20). [derived]
- Muzzle flash layer: a second sprite, always full-bright, started by the fire action and positioned from the weapon's current x and y each tic. It raises scene light for its duration: pistol 7 tics (one band), shotgun 4 then 3 (one then two bands), double barrel 5 then 4, chaingun 5 (one of two frames chosen by chance), rocket 3, 4, 4, 4, plasma 4 (one of two), BFG 11 then 6. [read]
- Screen tints (the palette file holds 14 palettes: 0 normal, 1-8 red, 9-12 gold, 13 green): damage uses a counter set to the health lost (after armour), capped at 100, falling 1 per tic; palette is 2 plus (counter plus 7) divided by 8, capped at 8 (counter 1-8 gives 2; 49 or more gives 8). A pickup adds 6 to a counter falling 1 per tic: counter 1-8 gives palette 10, 9-16 gives 11, 17 or more gives 12. Berserk keeps a red floor of 12 falling by 1 each 64 tics for 768 tics. The radiation suit (60 s, 2100 tics) is a green tint while more than 128 tics remain, then flickers 8 tics on, 8 off; damage and pickup tints take priority. Invulnerability (30 s) inverts the colours, light amplification (120 s) brightens, both blink in the last 128 tics; invisibility lasts 60 s. [read, derived]

## 13. Things

- A map thing is 10 bytes: x and y (16-bit map units), angle in degrees (rounded down to a multiple of 45), type (the editor number), options. Options: 1 easy (skills 1 and 2), 2 normal (skill 3), 4 hard (skills 4 and 5), 8 ambush (deaf), 16 multiplayer only (not spawned in a single-player game). Types 1-4 are player starts, 11 a deathmatch start (the first 10 are kept), 14 the teleport destination marker; types with the no-deathmatch flag (keys, the player) are skipped in deathmatch, and a "no monsters" option skips every monster and lost soul. An unknown type is a fatal error naming the type and position. [read]
- Spawning draws one gameplay random value per thing (its starting duration). Count of kills and items is taken from spawn flags. Floor things sit on the floor, hanging ones on the ceiling. [read]
- Edge case: in the first-episode game modes (not the sequel), meeting any of ten sequel-only monster types in the thing list ends thing loading for that map: the loop stops, not just skips. [read]
- Sizes, radius by height in units: player 16 by 56, zombie man, shotgun guy, imp, chaingunner and Wolf SS 20 by 56, demon and spectre 30 by 56, lost soul 16 by 56, cacodemon and pain elemental 31 by 56, baron and hell knight 24 by 64, mancubus 48 by 64, arachnotron 64 by 64, revenant and arch-vile 20 by 56, cyberdemon 40 by 110, spider mastermind 128 by 100, barrel 10 by 42, solid decorations 16, health and ammo pickups 20 by 16. Health: zombie man 20, shotgun guy 30, imp 60, chaingunner 70, demon and spectre 150, lost soul 100, cacodemon and pain elemental 400, baron 1000, hell knight 500, mancubus 600, arachnotron 500, revenant 300, arch-vile 700, cyberdemon 4000, spider mastermind 3000, barrel 20. Speeds in units per tic: 8 for most, 10 demon and revenant, 12 spider kinds, 15 arch-vile, 16 cyberdemon. [read]

## 14. The sequel (1.7)

Source: the sequel's version 1.7 source, a French-language build, compared with the version 1.10 release of the first game.

**The frame.** Version 1.10 already contains all of the sequel's content. Comparing the two sources field by field shows no difference in any of the 137 object kinds, any of the 966 animation states, the weapon table (including the double barrel), the 102 line-special trigger cases, the switch cases, or the sector specials. So sections 1 to 13 above hold for the sequel as written; this section lists what is new relative to the original game's data and what differs between the two source versions. [measured by table and case comparison]

**New monsters** (sizes in 13). All spawn only in the sequel's game mode (a map of the first game that lists one stops its thing loading, see 13). Behaviour in outline, read from the state tables and attack actions:
- Former human commando (editor type 65): hitscan bursts like the chaingun.
- Hell knight (69): the baron's behaviour with the baron's fireball; the two do not hurt each other.
- Revenant (66): a fast fist attack, and a slow missile (speed 10, damage 10 times a 1 to 8 roll) that steers towards its target.
- Mancubus (67): three fireballs per volley (speed 20, damage 8 times a 1 to 8 roll), two of them angled aside.
- Arachnotron (68): plasma shots (speed 25, damage 5 times a 1 to 8 roll).
- Pain elemental (71): spits lost souls instead of fireballs, none if more than 20 already live on the level; releases three more, left, behind and right of it, when it dies.
- Arch-vile (64): approaches, raises its aim, then a flame appears on the target after a delay, doing 20 direct damage and 70 blast damage, and the target is thrown up by 1000 divided by its mass units per tic; while chasing it brings any nearby corpse back to life.
- Wolf SS (84), the brain, the shooter and its targets (88, 89, 87) for the last boss map, and the Commander Keen kind (72, killed to open a tagged door). [read]

**New weapon, the double barrel** (also called the super shotgun; weapon slot after the shotgun). [read]
- Uses 2 shells per shot; it is chosen automatically when out of other ammo only with more than 2 shells; with the shotgun key pressed while owning it, it is selected first in this mode (not the plain shotgun).
- 20 pellets per shot, each 5, 10 or 15 damage; each pellet's sideways error is up to plus or minus 11.2 degrees and its vertical slope error up to plus or minus 0.12 (about 7 degrees), both with a triangular spread; the aim comes from the same three-ray search as the shotgun.
- Held cycle 57 tics (0.61 shots per second): 3 tics, the shot, then 54 tics of reload frames (a check that more ammo exists, open, load, close); after a release 5 more tics before ready. The flash is 5 then 4 tics and lifts the scene light one band, then two.
- The mega sphere (200 health and 200 armour) is a sequel-mode pickup only; in the first game's modes it is ignored. [read]

**Line and sector specials.** No difference from section 4 and 5: no new numbers, speeds or waits. The sequel's boss-map triggers differ in which kills open what: on map 7 the last mancubus lowers floors tagged 666 and the last arachnotron raises floors tagged 667; the Keen kind lowers tagged doors; the last-boss brain on its map exits on death after a spawn-cube sequence. [read]

**Map naming and progression.** Sequel maps are named MAP01 to MAP32 (two digits); the first game's are ExMy (episode 1 to 3 in 1.7, with a fourth in 1.10; maps 1 to 9). Sequel order is straight 1 to 30, 30 ends the game. A secret exit on map 15 goes to 31, a secret exit on map 31 goes to 32, and a normal exit from 31 or 32 returns to 16 (so after 31 or 32 the next map is 16 either way); a secret exit on a game without MAP31 is treated as a normal one. In the first game the secret map is 9, reached by the secret exit from any other map of the episode and returning to map 4 (episode 1), 6 (episode 2) or 7 (episode 3), and 3 in the fourth episode of 1.10; map 8 ends each episode. The game also refuses to start in sequel mode when MAP01 is missing. [read]

**Intermission and text screens.**
- Stats screen: the sequel has no world map; it shows one fixed background picture (INTERPIC) and, after the counting, an "entering" label for 4 seconds, except when the next map is 31. The first game's uses a map picture per episode with marks for visited levels and a blinking pointer. Par times are one value per map for the sequel (32 values) and a nine-by-three set for episodes. [read]
- Text screens (typed at one character per 3 tics, held 250 tics after the last character, music "read_m"): after maps 6, 11, 20 and 30, and after leaving map 15 or 31 by the secret exit. Backgrounds are floor pictures (SLIME16 after 6, RROCK14 after 11, RROCK07 after 20, RROCK17 after 30, RROCK13 after 15 secret, RROCK19 after 31 secret). After map 30's text comes the character cast sequence. The first game's end-of-episode texts use FLOOR4_8, SFLR6_1 and MFLR8_4 (and MFLR8_3 in the fourth episode of 1.10) with a victory tune; 1.10 adds twelve extra texts and level names for two map packs that 1.7 lacks. The text itself is identical between the two versions for every string both contain. [read, measured]

**Demo header.** The same 13-byte header and 4-byte commands as section 8; only the version number differs (107 in 1.7, 110 in 1.10). A mismatched version stops 1.7 with an error and makes 1.10 skip the demo with a message. [read]

**What differs between the two versions and matters to a port.** [read]
- A lost soul striking the floor in 1.10 reverses its vertical motion and bounces off; in 1.7 its vertical motion is set to zero before the reversal, so it does not rebound from floors (ceilings behave alike in both). This is a behaviour difference in a monster that appears in both games.
- The search for the next higher floor around a sector keeps at most 20 neighbours; 1.10 stops there and prints a warning, 1.7 has no limit and would overrun its list.
- The first game's fourth episode (map pictures, sky, texts, boss kills on maps 6 and 8) and a separate "retail" mode exist only in 1.10; 1.7 has three modes as flags. The attract loop has 6 steps in 1.7 and 7 in 1.10 with retail.
- 1.7 looks for data files by name in the order doom2f.wad, doom.wad, doom1.wad and ships French screen strings by default, the English set being present but switched off at build time; 1.10 recognises more names (the retail file, the two map packs and the French sequel).
- 1.7 refuses the god-mode cheat on the hardest skill; 1.10 allows it.
- 1.7 carries platform code for DOS sound and video cards, NeXT, SGI, X11 and a head-tracker device whose commands are added to the keyboard's, limited to 25 forward and 24 sideways; none of it affects the simulation. [read]

**What does not change.** Movement constants, friction, aiming, the random table and its use, the command layout, the network packet and the rendering rules are the same in both versions. [measured by comparison]
