# Common settings: one name for what the games share

WORKING AREA (deleted when porting ends). The audit Niclas asked for on 2026-11-22 (plan step 3): every
console setting and command of Quake, QuakeWorld (the stock client and server, and the modern client
and server inventoried in `client-inventory.md` and `server-inventory.md`) and Quake II (the
`q2-ledger-*.md` ledgers), matched across the games by meaning, each common one given ONE NAME OF
OURS in the lowest layer its games share, every game's spelling a synonym in that game's layer
(`docs/porting-games.md` § One name, one meaning). The missing count only goes down: a PR that
lifts a row flips it here.

How it was built: every name of the five reference sources (2,436 names; 2,578 name-and-game
pairs: 1,949 settings and 629 commands) read from the ledgers and inventories; each matched to
our setting by our registrations, the synonym tables (`client/cvar_layers.cpp`, the servers'
`kSettingNames`) and the ledgers' notes; names that differ between the games paired by what they do
(each pair settled by both games' descriptions, listed in the table's note). A name only one game
family has is listed at the end and is not a lift: it lives in its game (or a gap thread builds
it). Per-element HUD settings of the modern client (1,405) are left out: no other game has them.
Pairs under two names are as good as the pairs found: a pair found later in the lists at the end
is added to the table first, then lifted.

Statuses: **one setting** every game's spelling reaches one setting of ours already; **lifted** made
one by this work, its batch named; **to lift** one thing on two settings, or one game's
alone in ours, with its batch (B2 to B5 below); **kept apart** two settings on purpose: another
meaning, unit, value set or the opposite direction (the note says which); **sound thread** the
sound settings, which the OpenAL thread lifts with its own work; **neither has it** a debug knob
neither client takes.

## The batches

- **B1 (done, this audit's PR):** QuakeWorld's spellings as synonyms (`scr_consize`, `scr_conalpha`,
  `gl_consolefont`, `r_drawparticles`, `vid_reload`, `cl_net_clientport`, `in_grab_windowed_mouse`),
  Quake II's `gl_swapinterval`, and `gl_modulate`, `gl_anisotropy`, `in_grab` and `net_clientport` made
  every game's.
- **B2 (done):** the camera behind you every game's: `cl_thirdperson` and `cl_thirdperson_angle`
  in the classic layer (a cheat; Quake's `chase_active` a synonym), its distance our `cam_dist`, the
  one a watched player is seen from (Quake's `chase_back`, Quake II's `cl_thirdperson_range` with
  its 60 a same row).
- **B3 (done):** the move packets a second are one setting, our `cl_c2spps`, with Quake II's
  `cl_maxpackets` a synonym and its 30 a same row. The duplicates (two mechanisms), `cl_shownet` (a
  debug print in the tail not built) and `gender` (each game's servers' own words) are kept apart.
- **B4, the screen:** the graphs and the 2D switch, built by the Quake gaps thread. The server
  browser's sort and ping rate are kept apart (another value set each).
- **B5, the server's names:** `cheats`, the recording's size, the port (with the Quake II server thread).
- **B7 (done, Niclas 2026-11-23): what a competition player tunes, every classic game's.** One
  game's settings that every game now takes, in the classic layer, kept across a game change and
  checked by the rulesets by the same names in every game: the flat walls and their colours
  (`r_drawflat`, `r_wallcolor`, `r_floorcolor`), the plain sky (`r_fastsky`, `r_skycolor`,
  `r_skyname`, `r_skyfog`), the liquids seen through (`r_wateralpha`, `r_slimealpha`, `r_lavaalpha`,
  `r_telealpha`), the skins (`teamskin`, `enemyskin`, `r_fullbrightskins`), `r_drawflame`,
  `r_powerupglow`, `crosshairimage`, the tints' strengths (`v_contentblend`, `v_damagecshift`,
  `v_quadcshift`, `v_pentcshift`, `v_suitcshift`, `v_ringcshift`, `gl_cshiftpercent`; these were
  already the classic layer's), and the weapon's place: QuakeWorld's `r_viewmodelsize`,
  `r_viewmodeloffset`, `r_viewmodellastfired` and Quake II's `cl_gun_x/y/z`, `cl_gunfov` (Quake II's
  90 a same row, Quake's the view's) and `cl_gunalpha` moved out of their games' layers; `cl_bobhead`
  is the engine's. Held by `tests/shooters_edge_cvar_layers_test.cpp`
  (`the_competitive_settings_are_every_classic_games`).
- **B8 (done, Niclas 2026-11-23): settings that differ only in their values.** A game's spelling
  is a MAPPED synonym (`Cvars::add_mapped_synonym`): each of its values names one of ours, setting it
  writes ours and prints both (`cl_predict 0 (cl_nopred: 1)`), asking it reads ours back in its
  words. Quake II's `cl_predict` on `cl_nopred` and `cl_gibs` on `cl_gibfilter`, each the other way
  round. Every kept-apart row was checked again: the rest differ in meaning or mechanism, are
  other clients' visuals, or need a value map by scale (`fixedtime` in milliseconds against
  `host_framerate` in seconds).
- **B6 dropped (Niclas, 2026-11-23):** the outlines and square specks are other clients' visuals,
  which are never ported: visuals come from classic enhance. Both rows are kept apart.
