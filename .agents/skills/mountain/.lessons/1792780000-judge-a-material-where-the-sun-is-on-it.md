title: Judge a material where the sun is on it, and check the snow the view looks at
date: 2026-10-22

The first close views of the snow stood on spots the CPU march called sunlit, and every picture
came back dark blue: the spot was in the sun but the snow the camera looked at, a few metres on,
lay in a wood's shadow or on a slope turned from the sun. In shade the material's corduroy,
wrap, glare and glitter all vanish, so a plain and a snow picture match and the review learns
nothing. A view that judges a material must check what it LOOKS AT (the spot and points along
its heading), facing the sun, past the mountain and clear of the trees' shadows, and when no spot
passes it should take the best one rather than the middle of the piste. Run the lab at a few
hours: one map's sun can leave every candidate in shade at one hour and none at another.
