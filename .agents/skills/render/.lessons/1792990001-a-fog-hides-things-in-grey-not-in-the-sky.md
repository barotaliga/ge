Closing a hazed surface into the sky luminance toward its horizon looks right for clear air,
but under a fall or a fog it painted the far mountain the clear sky's blue, while the sky above
stayed deep blue through snow that hid everything past 1.2 km. The owner caught it at a glance.
Droplets and flakes scatter every colour alike, so the light they put between the eye and what
they hide is grey, a white face's light under the sun and sky a little dimmed. The sky has to be
seen through the same veil (a slab over the eye) or the picture contradicts itself. When a
weather's look is new, put it beside the clear day's and ask what colour the hidden things fade to.
